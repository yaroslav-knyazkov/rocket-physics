## Q1. Motor-induced roll torque (solid rocket motors)

### 1.1 What exists in the literature

- Knacke, R. N., "Roll torques produced by fixed-nozzle solid rocket motors", J. Spacecraft and Rockets, Nov 1996 (doi 10.2514/3.26839; conference version doi 10.2514/6.1995-2874). Abstract (https://doi.org/10.2514/6.1995-2874), verbatim: "Several causes of roll torques, observed levels including statistical databases for several motors, a simple empirical approach for estimating magnitudes for conceptual and preliminary design, and a guide for steps in development and testing of solid rocket motors to measure roll torques are presented. Most of the data has been accumulated from exoatmospheric flight of the NASA solid controlled orbital and utility test vehicle (SCOUT) launch vehicle." "Roll torques generated by internal vortex flow and nozzle ablation are the two most prominent sources. Transient torques having peak magnitudes as high as 163 N-m (120 ft-lbf) have been observed in exoatmospheric flights. There is no known method of predicting solid rocket motor generated roll torques of these types." "Historical data indicate that smooth bore motors produce smaller roll torques than star grain or finned type grain motors."
  - Full text NOT obtained (paywall). The "simple empirical approach" formula is unknown to me. Keywords on https://arc.aiaa.org/doi/10.2514/6.1995-2874 include Vortex Flow, Ablation, Jet Damping Torque.
  - Numbering inconsistency between sources: Exa lists the roll paper as AIAA 1995-2874 and the thrust-misalignment paper as 1995-2873, but the thrust-misalignment journal PDF says "Presented as Paper 95-2874". Not resolved.
- Knacke, "Thrust misalignments of fixed-nozzle solid rocket motors", JSR 33(6), Nov-Dec 1996 (free PDF: http://www.rsandt.com/media/Thrust%20Misalignments%20of%20Fixed-Nozzle%20Solid%20Rocket%20Motors.pdf). Covers pitch/yaw, not roll: "both translational ... and angular ... result in pitch and yaw torque about the vehicle center of mass". Numbers: most upper-stage SRMs "designed to have less than a 0.25-deg thrust misalignment"; best "three sigma ... below 0.15 deg"; nozzle install "less than 0.05-deg three sigma"; Star 31 tailoff transient "590 N-m (435 ft-lbf) of vehicle pitch-up moment ... equivalent thrust misalignment was 0.28 deg". Time structure classes: A pseudo-steady (manufacturing tolerance), B slowly varying (case/nozzle distortion with pressure, unsymmetric erosion), C rapid transients (aft-dome strain, acoustic instability, throat chipping), D ignition transients (15-30 ms), E static unbalance growing with burn time, F vehicle static unbalance. "Flight data statistics indicate a pitch plane bias and a larger standard deviation in the pitch plane than in the yaw plane" (Star 31).
- Bisi, Scaccia, Barbagallo (ASI/ESA), IAC-08.C4.2.11 (https://iafastro.directory/iac/archive/browse/IAC-08/C4/2/2476/): VEGA; "Type II roll, according to Knacke"; "semi-empirical scaling law ... to provide a preliminary estimate of peak roll value"; "dependence of roll torque on the starred portion of propellant grain"; "roll can only occur during combustion time of starred region and ... the angular impulse is not so significant, in spite of high peak values." (abstract only; law not given.)
- Stella, Giangi, Nardecchia, Barbagallo, "Roll torque prediction in SRM: Practical applications", IAC 2012 (http://hdl.handle.net/11573/485308): roll torque "not an uncommon event"; "In most cases roll torque is stronger during the initial phase of flight when slots in the combustion chamber are still present"; "a model for prediction of roll torque in SRM is not yet clearly established"; CFD on X-259 and Castor I gives qualitative agreement in time variation.
- Shimada, Sekino, Fukunaga, J. Propulsion and Power 2009 (doi 10.2514/1.40281): 3D CFD of slotted grain with submerged nozzle; two quasi-steady solution groups (large vs small roll torque); computed torque "one-order higher than that observed in the real fight".
- Kosmann, Dionne, Klemetson, AIAA 78-1076 (doi 10.2514/6.1978-1076): flight database from 23 Burner II/IIA/Block 5D-1 stages (25 firings) with time-varying roll torque couple magnitudes and impulses, population means and 3-sigma; AEDC ground data "consistently underestimated" roll torque for spherical motors. Numbers not in abstract.
- LAPAN sounding rocket roll amplification paper (https://ejournal.brin.go.id/ijoa/article/download/12386/9694): roll rate amplified during burn despite cruciform tail wings; swirling exhaust-gas model; simulated peak 330 deg/s from 45 deg prefix, ~7x amplification vs ~7.5x in flight; "the higher ratio of the propellant slot the higher roll torque" (citing Stella 2012); cites Knacke 1996 for star/finned grains.
- 1965 NASA TVC study (http://hdl.handle.net/2060/19660001347): "Motor swirl is assumed to produce negligible roll torque" (design assumption, not measurement).
- van der Ha & Janssens, JGCD 2005 (doi 10.2514/1.3852): jet-damping and misalignment torques for a spinning spacecraft during SRM burn; about jet damping, not motor roll torque.

### 1.2 Magnitudes actually quoted

- Absolute: roll-torque transient peaks up to 163 N-m (120 ft-lbf) on SCOUT exoatmospheric stages (Knacke abstract). SCOUT motors range thrust 29 kN (FW-4S, Star 20A) to 756 kN (Algol III) (Table 1, thrust-misalignment paper); the abstract does not say which motor produced 163 N-m.
- As a fraction of T*d: no published effective roll moment arm or formula found. Any T*d number would be my arithmetic (ASSUMPTION).
- Time structure: described qualitatively as transient/peaked, tied to star/slot grain burn-out, internal vortex flow, and nozzle ablation. Bias-vs-random split for roll: not in accessible text. (For pitch/yaw Knacke gives a bias plus standard deviation.)
- STAR / Orbital ATK / Northrop Grumman catalog (https://cdn.northropgrumman.com/-/media/wp-content/uploads/NG-Propulsion-Products-Catalog.pdf, https://www.generalstaff.org/BBOW/Progs/FlySIM/Catalog_OATK_2016.pdf): returned excerpts showed only contents and generic data (burn time, impulse, masses, spin rates); no roll-torque limit seen. Not proven absent (PDFs not read in full).
- NASA SP-8114 / SP-8039 / SP-8041: not retrieved. Unknown.
- Amateur/HPR data on motor roll torque: nothing found.

### 1.3 Is it modelled as kappa*T*xi(t)?

No source found with that form. Knacke defines pitch/yaw thrust misalignment as an equivalent angle at the throat: M_cm = T(X_T - X_cm)(eps_T/57.3) + T(dZ_cm - Z_T) (Fig. 1 of thrust-misalignment paper), a T times lever-arm form. For roll, sources say there is "no known method of predicting" it and recommend measurement. A kappa*T*xi(t) parametrisation would be an engineering choice (ASSUMPTION), not a published model.

## Q2. Dryden vertical component (MIL-F-8785C vs MIL-HDBK-1797)

Primary specs not read directly. Source: MathWorks block doc (secondary, quotes both) https://www.mathworks.com/help/aeroblks/drydenwindturbulencemodelcontinuous.html (PSD formulas obtained by Firecrawl scrape of that page), cross-checked with https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_gusts and the existing repo note `dev/docs/latex/research/dryden_turbulence_model.md`. h in feet AGL, low altitude h < 1000 ft.

### 2.1 Scale lengths (low altitude)

| MIL-F-8785C | MIL-HDBK-1797 / 1797B |
| --- | --- |
| L_w = h ; L_u = L_v = h/(0.177+0.000823h)^1.2 | 2 L_w = h ; L_u = 2 L_v = h/(0.177+0.000823h)^1.2 |

So 8785C: L_w = h, L_v = L_u. HDBK: L_w = h/2, L_v = L_u/2; L_u identical. Wikipedia gives the HDBK form: "2L_w = h, L_u = 2L_v = ... At 1000 ft AGL, L_u = 2L_v = 2L_w = 1000 ft." The existing repo note section 3.1 (L_w = h, L_u = L_v) is the 8785C convention only.

### 2.2 Intensities (same in both)

sigma_w = 0.1 W20; sigma_u/sigma_w = sigma_v/sigma_w = 1/(0.177+0.000823h)^0.4. W20 = wind at 20 ft (6 m): 15 kt light, 30 kt moderate, 45 kt severe. At 1000 ft sigma_u = sigma_v = sigma_w = 0.1 W20 (Wikipedia). sigma_w does not depend on h; sigma_u = sigma_v >= sigma_w below 1000 ft.

### 2.3 PSD (temporal, V = airspeed; MathWorks table, as scraped)

- 8785C: Phi_w(omega) = sigma_w^2 (L_w/(pi V)) (1 + 3 (L_w omega/V)^2) / [1 + (L_w omega/V)^2]^2
- HDBK-1797/1797B: Phi_w(omega) = sigma_w^2 (2 L_w/(pi V)) (1 + 12 (L_w omega/V)^2) / [1 + 4 (L_w omega/V)^2]^2
- Phi_u (both): sigma_u^2 (2 L_u/(pi V)) / (1 + (L_u omega/V)^2)
- Phi_v row not captured; by pattern (ASSUMPTION) same as w with L_v, sigma_v.

Derived (my algebra, ASSUMPTION): substituting L' = L/2 (HDBK L_w) into the HDBK form gives 2L' = L, 12L'^2 = 3L^2, 4L'^2 = L^2, i.e. exactly the 8785C expression in L. So the factor-of-2 scale-length convention describes the same physical w spectrum; the two documents differ only in how L is defined, and mixing the HDBK L_w with the 8785C PSD (or vice versa) changes the spectrum. Supporting: Ichwan, IOP Conf. Ser. 1005 012017 (https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/1005/1/012017/pdf) reports a Simulink HDBK-1797 implementation matches the MathWorks block in u_g while the other components differ, "caused by different turbulence scale length definition for Lv and Lw". NASA N87-25281 (https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19870015848/downloads/19870015848.pdf): "The only difference in Lw between models is that Calspan uses a factor of two, so its scale is half the other model's value."

### 2.4 Medium/high altitude (h > 2000 ft), isotropic

MathWorks: 8785C L_u = L_v = L_w = 1750 ft; HDBK-1797/B L_u = 2L_v = 2L_w = 1750 ft (so L_v = L_w = 875 ft). sigma_u = sigma_v = sigma_w from a lookup table vs altitude and exceedance probability. 1000-2000 ft: linear interpolation. MathWorks: "recommend 1750 feet for the longitudinal turbulence scale length".

### 2.5 Axes, and 2D vertical-plane simulator

MathWorks (low altitude): "Longitudinal turbulence velocity, u_g, aligned along the horizontal relative mean wind vector. Vertical turbulence velocity, w_g, aligned with vertical." Lateral v_g is horizontal and perpendicular to the mean wind (inference; excerpt does not spell it out). Medium/high: isotropic, output in body axes.
Inference for a downrange-x / vertical-z plane: w is always in-plane; u is in-plane in proportion to the angle psi between mean wind and the plane (u cos psi); v is purely out-of-plane only if the mean wind lies in the plane, otherwise it also projects onto the plane (v sin psi). The existing repo note section 4.3 deferred w.

## Q3. Lumped descent model

- Summing drag areas is taught practice: Whitmore (Utah State lecture notes, https://mae-nas.eng.usu.edu/MAE_6530_Web/New_Course/launch_design/Section3.5.pdf and http://mae-nas.eng.usu.edu/MAE_5900_Web/5900/USLI_2010/Recovery/Section7.1.pdf): equations of motion with (F_drag)_parachute + (F_drag)_vehicle; V_terminal = sqrt(2 m g / [(C_D S_0)_parachute + (C_D S_0)_vehicle]); example vehicle drag area "2 x (C_D)_rocket x (A_ref) = 0.0111 m^2" added to chute drag area. These are lecture notes, not Knacke.
- Knacke, NWC TP 6575 (1991) (https://www.manuallib.com/download/named/59/para-publishing-nwc-tp-6575-parachute-recovery-systems-design-manual.pdf; DTIC ADA247666): TOC lists 5.2.2 "Forebody Wake Effect", 5.3.3 "Stability of a Parachute Body System", 5.4 inflation incl. 5.4.4 "Drag-Area Increase During Canopy Filling Process" and 5.4.6 "Methods for Calculating Parachute Opening Forces", 5.11 "Frequently Used Formulas". Only the TOC and Ch.1-2 were read; I cannot quote Knacke's treatment of summing body and canopy drag. The forebody-wake section title indicates the body wake can affect canopy drag (title only).
- OpenRocket: technical documentation section 4.2.5 (Niskanen, https://openrocket.sourceforge.net/techdoc.pdf), as quoted by https://hpr.fusionspace.co/physics/recovery.html: "the entire drag coefficient of the rocket is assumed to come from the deployed recovery devices"; parachute C_D default 0.8 (Hoerner); devices open fully at once (no filling time). So OpenRocket does not add airframe drag (contrast with Whitmore). I did not re-read the techdoc PDF for this sentence.
- RocketPy: drag area given as cd_s; airframe drag omitted in descent (FusionSpace: "The airframe's own drag is left out, as RocketPy leaves it out ... For a drogue whose drag area is close to the airframe's broadside area this is a real omission").
- What lumped point-mass models miss (FusionSpace write-up, secondary): no rotation/moment ("The drag acts at the center of mass"); attitude frozen at deployment; no pendulum; "Added mass is not modeled" (apparent mass quoted 5.6 kg on a 16.2 kg rocket, 15.9 kg on 20.8 kg; "changes no equilibrium descent rate, only the transient right after an opening"). Inflation: FusionSpace uses (C_D S)(t) = (C_D S)_0 min(1,(t-t_d)/t_f)^j (j=1 or 2, Knacke Table 5-6 fill constants); opening-shock coefficient C_x reported but unused. OpenRocket opens instantly. Two-body riser/shock-cord dynamics: not found in any lumped model I read. OpenRocket issue #1864 (https://github.com/openrocket/openrocket/issues/1864): reefed/two-stage chutes "have to be modeled with two recovery devices ... a second chute with a size calculated so the total drag of the two devices is the drag of the actual chute" (drag-area summation as user workaround).
- C_D spread: Apogee Rockets measurement (https://www.apogeerockets.com/education/downloads/Newsletter662_Large.pdf) 0.53-1.04 (0.72 suggested) for a 610 mm hex nylon chute; Knacke flat circular C_D0 0.75-0.80 on nominal area (via FusionSpace).

## Q4. Air-brake aerodynamics representation

- Whole-vehicle C_D table (b) is what the accessible sources use.
  - Farha, WVU MS thesis 2025 (https://researchrepository.wvu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=13583&context=etd): Fluent k-omega SST, 4 brake designs, Mach 0.2-1.2, 50% and 100% deployment, "tabulated collection of drag coefficient and CP results"; trajectory code uses bilinear interpolation in Mach and deployment, constant outside the Mach range. Reference area = body area 0.0194 m^2. Base C_D 0.469 (M0.2), 0.448 (M0.6), 0.628 (M1.0). 100%-deployed totals at M0.2: RB 0.788, EB 1.017, AB 1.109, FB 1.364; at M1.2 up to 2.134 (FB).
  - RocketPy docs (https://github.com/RocketPy-Team/RocketPy/blob/cb15a393/docs/user/airbrakes.rst): air-brakes drag coefficient as a function of deployment level and Mach (snippet truncated; reference-area convention not seen).
  - Liberty Univ. (https://digitalcommons.liberty.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3040&context=research_symp): CFD C_D(Mach); Mach 0.5 retracted ~0.50, deployed ~0.72; deployed results at M0.6-0.8 "don't fit the pattern" (simplified 2D brake geometry).
  - UNH HAAMSHR (https://scholars.unh.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1960&context=honors; poster https://media-gallery.unh.edu/media_file_download/2872/): tabulated C_D vs deployment, "Drag force evaluated to 800 N at 300 m/s", "Coefficient of drag evaluated to ~ 1.0"; servo angle via lookup table.
  - Hafner/eCon (https://econengineering.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Hafner-Zoltan-Aerodynamic-performance-investigationof-rocket-air-brakes.pdf): 85 CFD cases, deployment 0-100%, Mach 0.1-2.0; apogee 3182 m -> 2790 m.
- Flat-plate component form (a), used as a simplification: Univ. of Georgia (https://region2.aiaastudentconference.org/collection/2025/manuscripts/98650.pdf): "the rounded edges of the flat plates are averaged into a larger rectangle"; F = 1/2 rho v^2 C_d A for both rocket and brakes; constant C_d assumed in the model, "lookup tables will be utilized from CFD results in implementation". SA Cup 2018 report (https://www.soundingrocket.org/uploads/9/0/6/4/9064598/55_project_report.pdf): 110.4 cm^2 area gives 219.9 N at 250 m/s and 32.6 N at 100 m/s (ratio 6.75 vs (250/100)^2 = 6.25; my arithmetic).
- Typical brake coefficient: Farha defines C'_D = F_D/(q_c A_brake) with isentropic q_c; finds ~0.75 at 50% deployment and ~1.00 at 100% across designs/Mach (Table 5.2: 0.92-1.09 for 100% at M0.2-0.8, 1.34 for FB at M1.0); recommends 1.00 if brake height exceeds boundary-layer thickness, ~0.75 if inside the boundary layer or non-orthogonal; cites a 1958 NACA speed-brake study. Flat plate normal to flow C_D 1.17 and open hemispherical cup 1.42 appear in the OpenRocket techdoc via Hoerner (relayed by https://hpr.fusionspace.co/physics/recovery.html).
- Effect on CP / normal force: Farha Table 3.3 static margin (calibers): M0.2 base 3.37, EB50 3.28, FB50 2.49, RB50 3.49, AB50 2.46, EB100 3.38, FB100 2.89, RB100 3.36, AB100 0.25; M0.4 AB100 1.16. Conclusion: stability "largely upheld for air brakes positioned aft of the center-of-gravity location"; AB100 low-Mach instability due to wake over the fins moving CP forward (fix: place brakes further aft). Hafner: "Static stability of rocket remains unaffected" for their configuration. So CP shift is design- and Mach-dependent. C_N change was not tabulated in any source I read.
- Component build-up inside OpenRocket/Barrowman for brakes: not found. No OpenRocket air-brake plugin documentation found. OpenRocket user guide mentions custom aerodynamic data via CSV lookup tables from wind tunnel/CFD (https://openrocket.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/advanced_flight_simulation.html, snippet only). Cowboy Rocketworks 2018 (https://www.soundingrocket.org/uploads/9/0/6/4/9064598/54_project_report.pdf): baseline CFD drag "within 10%" of OpenRocket.
